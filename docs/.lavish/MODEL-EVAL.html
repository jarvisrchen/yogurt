The loop

The script is the fixed input and the ground truth. Each trial goes through the real product path: speaker → ScreenCaptureKit → STT under test → enhance with the LLM under test. Every meeting row remembers what produced it, so the judge never has to guess which run was which.

02

Run one trial

Repeat this once per configuration you want to compare. The UI and the terminal alternate; nothing is automated across the two on purpose, so the run goes through exactly what a user does.

1Settings

Pick the engine and model

Choose STT and LLM in Settings, or start the backend with YOGURT_LLM_* env vars.

2You

Put on headphones

Keeps the mic from double-capturing the speaker as me. System audio is still captured.

3UI

Start a new meeting

Recording begins, stt_engine is stamped.

4Terminal

just eval-play

Speaks the script, ~5 minutes. Watch the lines scroll; wait for done.

5UI

Stop, let enhance finish

llm_model is stamped once the summary lands.

6You

Copy the meeting URL

/meeting/<id>/post from the address bar. Bare ids work too.

Why headphones

System audio is captured through ScreenCaptureKit whatever the output device, so the them channel is always clean. An open mic next to the speakers records the same audio a second time as me, which is the live-transcript duplication already logged in docs/TODO.md, and it would skew the comparison.

Compare two trials

scripts/eval/compare.shreport to stdout, redirect to keep it
# URLs or bare ids, in any mix
just eval-compare http://localhost:7878/meeting/<id-a>/post <id-b> > report.md

# print the assembled prompt instead of judging (paste it into another model)
PROMPT_ONLY=1 scripts/eval/compare.sh <a> <b>

# pick the judge model, or swap the ground truth
JUDGE_MODEL=opus scripts/eval/compare.sh <a> <b>
SCRIPT=scripts/eval/standup.txt scripts/eval/compare.sh <a> <b>

What the judge grades

Transcript accuracy matters most

Words, numbers, names, dates versus the script. Every wrong number or name is called out: five percent, zero point three, the fifth not the fourth, Priya, Jordan.

Summary faithfulness

Does the summary say only things the conversation said? Every hallucination or misattribution is listed.

Coverage

The decision, the disagreement and its resolution, the self-correction, and every action item with owner and date, as an A / B table.

Usefulness

Would someone who missed it be correctly briefed? Structure, concision, and no tangents as headlines (the espresso machine is bait).

The report ends with a scorecard table, a one-paragraph verdict naming the better run, and the single most damaging error in each run.

04

Better voices

The voices that ship with macOS are the compact variants and sound robotic. Apple's Premium and Enhanced variants are free downloads and say can use them; play.sh prefers them automatically once they are on disk.

SpeakerPreference orderFallback
A (Sam)Ava (Premium) → Zoe (Premium) → Samantha (Enhanced)Samantha
B (Dan)Daniel (Enhanced) → Tom (Enhanced)Daniel
Install once

System Settings → Accessibility → Spoken Content → System Voice → Manage Voices..., tick the voices above, wait for the download. Confirm with say -v '?' | grep -i premium. Siri voices are not exposed to say. Override any time: A_VOICE="Zoe (Premium)" just eval-play.

05

Writing another script

Keep the format: A: and B: lines are spoken, PAUSE <seconds> is silence, # is a comment. Anything else makes play.sh stop with the offending line.

  • Spell numbers and dates out the way a person says them. That is what the STT hears and what the judge checks against.
  • Give the judge something to grade: at least one decision, one disagreement, one correction, and action items with owners and dates.
  • Add one tangent. A summary that headlines it is a useful failure signal.
  • Keep it under ~700 words so a trial stays around five minutes.

Play it with just eval-play path/to/new.txt and compare with SCRIPT=path/to/new.txt just eval-compare a b.
